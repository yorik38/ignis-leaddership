Commercial transformation

Rethink the business model.Use AI where it helps.

Test where governed AI could support a move from develop-and-sell to build-and-operate, or make new services and revenue models viable. Start with the commercial case and operating obligations.

The strategic choice

Decide what the business should own and operate. Then test where AI helps.

For a developer considering a longer role in an asset, the question is bigger than productivity. Ownership, operations and new offers bring different responsibilities, economics and risks.

A disciplined test

Begin with the value the model would create and what the business is prepared to deliver. Then ask whether AI can make information, decisions or ongoing support more repeatable without obscuring accountability.

AI is an enabler, not the reason to change models. An attractive idea still needs a commercial case and a credible way to operate it.

Models to examine

Change the role the business plays, not just the speed of its work.

These are strategic hypotheses for developers and IPPs to test—not client results or recommendations to change models.

/

Develop and operate

If the business intends to retain and operate assets, test where AI could help maintain evidence, surface exceptions and support decisions across the longer lifecycle. Operating responsibility stays with people.

/

Offer ongoing services

Explore whether project and operational knowledge could support a useful service beyond development. Test customer value, delivery obligations and a viable route to revenue before designing the system.

/

Scale specialist capability

Examine whether repeatable analysis, monitoring or decision support could extend the reach of a scarce team without turning its judgement into an unchecked automated promise.

Five viability tests

Test the model before specifying the technology.

A longer role in an asset or a new offer must work commercially and operationally—not merely look possible with AI.

/

Value

What customer, investor or project need does the model serve, and how would we know?

/

Right to play

Which rights, relationships, expertise and data can the business credibly bring?

/

Responsibility

What must the business own, operate, control or stand behind—and who remains accountable?

/

Economics

Do capital, delivery cost, risk and potential revenue make the model worthwhile?

/

Route to market

Who will buy, fund or use it, and can the team sustain the offer or operation?
