The real comparison

Which situation sounds like your team?

Do not ask which option sounds more advanced. Ask where the work is repeatable, where it changes by opportunity, and which decisions must remain visible and human.

Platform likely leads

High volume.
Stable offer.

  • The product or service is already defined.
  • Questions recur across RFPs, DDQs or security reviews.
  • Approved answers and evidence can be reused.
  • The pressure is search, coordination, consistency and throughput.

Start by evaluating established software. Configure it around trusted content, contributors and review.

Bespoke workflow likely leads

Complex pursuit.
Changing offer.

  • The solution is shaped during the bid.
  • Evidence comes from many systems and specialists.
  • Risk, price, delivery and commitments interact.
  • The pressure is analysis, orchestration and decision quality.

Start by mapping the work. Design one governed workflow before choosing the components.

Both may be right

Mixed portfolio.
Different jobs.

  • Recurring responses sit beside strategic pursuits.
  • A platform solves part of the production burden.
  • Complex opportunities still cross functional boundaries.
  • The team needs one operating model across both.

Use each where it is strongest. Connect the platform to the wider bid workflow without duplicating it.

The decision rule

Remove operational burden without hiding the commercial decisions that determine whether the bid can win and be delivered.

What the bespoke workflow is

A governed chain of work, not a better writing prompt.

An agentic bid workflow connects bounded tasks around the live pursuit. Agents prepare the baseline work from approved sources. People decide the strategy, shape the solution, judge risk and approve every commitment.

Today

Your best people carry the system.

They search for evidence, chase contributors, reconcile versions, rebuild analysis and remember what was agreed.

With a governed workflow

The system carries the baseline work.

It assembles evidence, exposes gaps, coordinates inputs and prepares checks so people can focus on the client, solution and commercial choices.

01

Frame

Extract the requirements, obligations and unknowns. People set the bid strategy, priorities and boundaries.

02

Research

Find relevant evidence, prior work and external context. Show sources, confidence and missing information.

03

Develop

Coordinate specialist inputs, test themes and prepare material around the agreed solution, not a generic answer.

04

Assure

Check compliance, claims, contradictions and dependencies. Named people approve risk, price and commitments.

05

Learn

Measure time, review effort and quality. Improve the workflow and evidence base after each use.

What it should improve

More time for the work that wins.

  • Less manual research, chasing, checking and assembly.
  • Earlier visibility of evidence gaps, contradictions and decisions.
  • More consistent use of approved material with traceable sources.
  • More senior time for client insight, solution design and commercial judgement.
  • A working method the team can test and improve over time.

The durable asset

Build the workflow. Use software where it helps.

Bespoke does not mean building every technology component. It means making the team’s way of working explicit enough to operate, govern and improve, even as individual tools change.

Within the agreed engagement scope, the workflow, controls and learning are designed to sit with the organisation rather than remain inside an adviser’s prompt history.

  • Process and decision mapHow work, evidence and authority move today and in the target workflow.
  • Human-agent operating designWhat agents prepare or coordinate, where they stop and who remains accountable.
  • Approved-source and control modelWhat the workflow may use, how provenance appears and how exceptions are handled.
  • Evaluation and operating artefactsThe test cases, quality criteria, logs and playbook used to judge and run the workflow.
  • Team learningThe practical knowledge needed to review, challenge and improve the workflow.

The retainer question

If support continues, does the client still own the workflow?

Yes, if the engagement is designed properly. A retainer can support monitoring, evaluation, improvement and the next connection without making the operating method disappear when the support stops.

That does not mean every cost ends. The workflow may still rely on third-party software, model APIs, cloud infrastructure or support services. Exact ownership, licensing and usage rights belong in the contract. The important distinction is that the organisation understands how the work runs, where the controls sit and what good looks like instead of renting an unexplained outcome.

Questions leaders ask

Before you choose the route.

Does Responsive already provide an agentic bid workflow?

It provides substantial agentic and workflow capability within Strategic Response Management. Its public material covers governed knowledge, requirements analysis, response workflows, auditability, reporting, integrations and configurable agents. Ignis does not claim that platforms only write. The difference is where the work starts: with your operating model, the wider commercial process and an independent decision about which technology belongs where.

Should we replace Loopio, Responsive, AutogenAI, QorusDocs or Qvidian?

Not necessarily. An established RFP platform may remain the right component for content management, response production and collaboration. Ignis can design the organisation-specific workflow, decision rights, controls and integrations around it.

What is a bespoke agentic bid workflow?

It is a designed chain of work in which AI agents research, analyse, coordinate and prepare outputs from approved sources while named people retain responsibility for strategy, solution design, commercial judgement and commitments.

Is Ignis another RFP software subscription?

No. Ignis works with the team to map a complex bid workflow, decide where agents can safely help, and build and test one governed workflow using approved evidence, existing systems and named human decisions.

What does the organisation retain after the engagement?

The intended durable assets include the process and control design, decision rights, approved-source map, evaluation criteria, operating artefacts and team learning developed within the agreed engagement scope.

Does an Ignis retainer mean the workflow is still rented?

A retainer can support operation, evaluation and improvement. It does not change the aim of embedding the workflow and governance in the organisation. Third-party software, cloud and model charges may still continue, and exact ownership and usage rights are defined in the engagement contract.

Can a bespoke agentic bid workflow use our existing technology?

Yes. The first workflow should normally use the documents, cloud environment, enterprise tools and approved knowledge the team already has. Replacement is justified only where the evidence shows it is necessary.
