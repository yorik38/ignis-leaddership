Briefing

Welcome to the first Ignis Market Monitor. I'm genuinely happy to put it out. I care a lot about the energy transition, offshore and onshore, and I started this research to get a clearer picture for myself: what is actually moving in power prices, tenders, grid and deals, and what that means for the people developing projects.

I hope it saves you some time and gives you something useful for the months ahead. This research is also the intelligence that feeds the agentic workflow I am building for my own consulting work, so every figure is sourced and every judgement is marked. If something is wrong or missing, please tell me.

Yorik Tisseau, Ignis

For CEOs and commercial and bid leads at offshore wind developers and at UK onshore wind, solar and BESS developers and IPPs. Every figure is from a numbered public source listed at the end. Our own estimates and judgements are marked ASSUMPTION; items we could not confirm are marked UNVERIFIED.

1

The broader market: why power doubled

Gas sets the price, and gas has nearly tripled. Gas-fired plant still sets the marginal GB power price much of the time 1011. NBP gas was about 217p/therm on 18 October, up 168% on a year earlier 12. At 50% efficiency, the fuel cost of a gas plant rose from about £53 to £130/MWh over the year, roughly the whole rise in spot power (Figure 1; Ignis calculation from 1213).

The trigger is the Iran conflict and the Strait of Hormuz. Day-ahead gas jumped in early March as the conflict escalated 10, Hormuz disruption drew LNG cargoes away from the UK in Q2 11, and US and Iran exchanges lifted day-ahead NBP 34% in July 14. Stalled peace talks kept prices rising in September 15; shipping attacks, Norwegian outages and Asian LNG at $29.61/MMBtu added more in the week to 13 October 16.

Low storage makes winter the risk. The EU started winter with storage 72% full, against 83% a year earlier, which GIE calls historically low; the UK, with little storage of its own, tracks the continental fill 21718. In a cold winter ENTSOG sees EU storage falling to about 11% by the end of March 1718. NESO's June early view still expects GB electricity margins to be adequate 19.

Wind drives the swings; carbon is minor. Day-ahead power rose from £130 to £160/MWh over September on a sharp fall in wind, and strong wind took next-day power to £33.89 on 9 October 1615. UK ETS allowances were £65.92/t on 13 October against a 2026 reference price of £49.41/t 1620; ASSUMPTION at about 0.37 tCO₂/MWh for a CCGT, that adds only £3 to £6/MWh.

Forwards put the premium in 2027. On 16 October Summer-27 power settled at £127.36/MWh, up from £83.00 at the end of July; Summer-28 was £80.69 and Summer-29 £72.80 142. Most of these figures come from broker and consultancy notes, not exchange data.

2

European offshore wind

Two large rounds dominate this quarter, AR8 (UK) and AO10 (FR), with rule changes elsewhere and a busy month for stake sales. Fixed-bottom and floating are on different economics, so we take them separately where the sources allow.

UK: AR8 offshore against the cap

Fixed-bottom. The AR8 administrative strike price for offshore wind is £113/MWh (2024 prices) 21. AR7 cleared 8,245 MW at £91.20 (England and Wales) and £89.49 (Scotland) 22, about 19% below that cap (Figure 2; Ignis calculation from 2122). Floating. Floating and the new "other deepwater" category (foundations for deep water that do not technically float) bid in their own pot, Pot 4, apart from fixed-bottom in Pot 3, against a cap of £271/MWh; AR7 cleared 192.5 MW at £216.49, about 20% below it 2122. DESNZ's reasoning is that deep-water wind has not yet been built at the scale needed to show which technologies are cheapest, so the rules should not block new designs 23; ASSUMPTION a separate pot also spares them a straight price fight with mature fixed-bottom. Government will see the bids in Pots 1 to 4 before it sets each budget, but the stated test is value for money for consumers, not deliverability: it will only add budget for bids that are strong value 21. So AR8 (UK) still awards on price, while AO10 (FR) scores proof of deliverability directly. The budget notice and Notice of Auction are due on 19 November, with results expected 15 to 17 February 2027 56.

France: AO10 bids are in

Bids for about 10 GW across 11 projects closed at noon on 12 October 324. By our arithmetic from the DGEC project list, that is about 5.25 GW of fixed-bottom (four projects) and 4.9 GW of floating (seven projects) 25. CRE has 12 weeks to rank the offers (to 4 January 2027, 18 January at the latest) and winners are expected in February 2027 342627. The round sits under a €63bn, 25-year support scheme approved by the European Commission in July 28.

Rules that decide it. Offers are marked out of 100: price 70, robustness of the contractual and financial set-up 18, carbon footprint 6, European content 6, and a bid below 11 of the 18 robustness points is eliminated 31. There is no cap per project; the target is a capacity-weighted average of €100/MWh across all winners, and if it is exceeded CRE drops projects, latest grid dates and highest prices first 3132. ASSUMPTION Cheap fixed-bottom bids make room for floating. Bidders need at least 500 MW of offshore wind and €1bn of offshore investment, so newcomers need a partner 31. On floating appetite, Spinergie estimates around 330 floaters for the tender 29, and TotalEnergies' chief executive has said the company is no longer interested in floating wind 33. ASSUMPTION For bidders, the time to February is when partner governance, the first 90 days after award, supply-chain slots and the financing plan get prepared. I went through the scoring in detail in Signal Issue 1, AO10 bids submitted: why proof, not price, will decide France's offshore wind round: read it on ignisleadership.com.

The wider European picture

Beyond AR8 (UK) and AO10 (FR), little is being awarded this quarter; most of the movement is in rules. Germany's cabinet adopted a draft amendment to its offshore wind act on 2 September: tenders run subsidy-free first, with a two-sided CfD if no bids come in, 2,000 to 4,800 MW a year and a 35-year operating period; it is not yet law 3435. The Netherlands, which has awarded offshore wind without subsidy since 2018, is paying support again after its 1 GW Nederwiek I-A tender drew no bids in October 2025 3637. For the 2 GW IJmuiden Ver Gamma sites it offers a temporary, SDE++-style subsidy that tops up revenue when power prices fall below the winning bid; in June the maximum bid was raised from €104 and €103/MWh to €117 (Gamma-A) and €116/MWh (Gamma-B), because a proposed grid charge on producers adds cost uncertainty 383940. Gamma-A bids run from 26 November to 10 December, with a subsidy-free auction alongside, and two-sided CfDs are planned for later rounds 3941. Belgium is relaunching its first Princess Elisabeth Zone tender, cancelled in July 2025, with one two-sided CfD on all output and no maximum strike price: the cap is removed, not raised or lowered, because the government judged a preset cap could deter bidders; the lowest strike price wins 424344. The relaunch still needs EU state-aid approval 45. For reference, Denmark's August tender awarded 1.8 GW to Vattenfall at €67.42 and €72.50/MWh under two-sided CfDs 3446. In finance, Skyborn signed €2.2bn of project finance for the 975 MW Gennaker farm in the German Baltic, backed by corporate PPAs with Amazon (600 MW) and Uniper (100 MW) 34.

3

UK onshore: the last mile to financial close

For onshore wind, solar and BESS developers becoming IPPs, the market move lands on four commercial calls at once.

Route to market: one sealed bid against a fixed cap. The AR8 caps are £75/MWh for solar and £92 for onshore wind (2024 prices); AR7 cleared at £65.23 and £72.24, 13% and 21% below them (Figure 2) 2122. The budget notice and Notice of Auction are due on 19 November, and results are expected 15 to 17 February 2027 56.

What "Pots 1 to 4 versus Pot 5" means. AR8 sorts technologies into five pots, each with its own budget: Pot 1 is solar (over 5 MW); Pot 2 is onshore wind and remote island wind (over 5 MW); Pot 3 is offshore wind; Pot 4 is floating and other deep-water offshore wind; Pot 5 is everything else, such as tidal stream, wave, geothermal, hydro, anaerobic digestion, landfill and sewage gas, and biomass or energy from waste with CHP 21. In Pots 1 to 4 each project may submit only one sealed bid: one strike price, one capacity and one set of target dates (Rules 13.1 and 13.3) 50. In Pot 5 a project may submit up to four "flexible bids" with different capacities or delivery dates, and the auction can fall back on a smaller or later variant if the first does not fit the budget (Rule 13.7) 50. Pots 1 to 4 are also the pots where Government sees the anonymised bids that do not fit the budget; it will only add budget for bids it judges strong value for money, and it weighs technologies against each other, so more budget for one pot may mean less for another 21. ASSUMPTION For a solar or onshore wind developer this means one shot: capacity, delivery year and price have to be settled before bidding, and a bid priced just outside the budget depends on Government rating it good value against offshore and other onshore bids.

How AR8 differs from earlier rounds. Flexible bids are not new: since the first round in 2014, applicants could submit alternative bids with a smaller capacity or a later delivery date, up to ten then and up to four by AR7 5152. AR7 removed them for fixed-bottom offshore wind only, and it was also the first round in which Government looked at anonymised bids that did not fit the budget, again for fixed-bottom offshore only 5253. AR8 extends both rules to every technology in Pots 1 to 4: solar, onshore and remote island wind, and floating and deep-water offshore now get a single sealed bid with one price, one capacity (no more than the figure in the application) and one set of target dates, and Government sees the bids that fall outside the budget before deciding whether to add money 215054.

Grid: Gate 2 in plain words

What it is. Connections reform replaced "first come, first served" with a queue re-ordered on readiness (checked against evidence such as land rights and the planning route) and on strategic alignment with the Government's Clean Power 2030 plan 5556. A project that passes gets Gate 2: an offer with a confirmed connection date, point of connection, works and cost. A project that does not gets Gate 1: its old dates become indicative only, any cash security is refunded, and it can reapply in a later window 55. Gate 2 projects are split by when the plan needs them: Phase 1 for connections needed in 2026 to 2030, Phase 2 for 2031 to 2035 55. Each phase comes in two steps: NESO issues transmission offers first (projects on the transmission network and large projects on local networks), then the distribution network operators (DNOs, such as NGED) issue offers to smaller projects on their networks, which need NESO's transmission information first 579.

Deadlines set or agreed by Ofgem 5758.

What happens on 31 October. It is NESO's deadline for the last Phase 1 transmission offers, about 62 of them. The original deadline was 30 September; Ofgem extended it because National Grid Electricity Transmission (NGET, the transmission owner in England and Wales) was late with the engineering inputs NESO needs, and about 25 projects asked to receive their offer later 57. Phase 2 and distribution deadlines did not move 57. The NGED dates are separate: they are its Phase 1 distribution batches, each group of grid supply points with its own issue date and acceptance deadline 9.

What the developer must do after an offer lands. For a NESO Gate 2 offer: sign within 90 days, then place securities within 30 days of signing; the first payment can be anything from zero to several million pounds, depending on how much the network owner is spending, and is updated every six months 55. For an NGED distribution offer: accept by the batch deadline, about six weeks after issue (Batch 5 was issued on 14 September, with acceptance by 26 October) 9; other DNOs set their own timelines 55. If a Gate 2 offer lapses, NESO must issue a residual Gate 1 offer within 6 weeks 58. Once signed, the agreement carries milestones worked back from the connection date, starting with beginning consents (M1), securing consents (M2) and securing land rights (M3); a project that misses the early milestones is terminated unless an exception applies 59. Cutting the capacity declared at application triggers cancellation charges 55.

What Figure 3 shows. Of the 604 GW in the transmission queue not yet built, 20.8 GW carries a Gate 2 label, about 295 GW Gate 1, and about 288 GW is "not yet designated", our label for rows whose Gate field is still blank (Ignis calculation from the 16 October TEC Register 60). The field only fills once an agreement is countersigned, so Gate 2 looks small partly because offers are still being issued and signed: about four projects a week have gained the label since 25 August 61. By 10 June, NESO and the DNOs had issued Gate 2 offers to 713 projects (37 GW), including distribution offers the register does not carry 61. By technology, solar (including solar with storage, 186 GW) and storage only (179 GW) are the largest; storage has the most in Gate 1 (113 GW) and in Gate 2 (12.5 GW); onshore wind including hybrids is 69 GW, 1.5 GW of it at Gate 2; offshore wind is 97 GW, three quarters not yet designated 60. ASSUMPTION A blank or Gate 1 row is not a verdict on a project; the offer letter is what counts.

Method and data notes

Data checked: market data 16 to 18 October 2026; storage, offshore and procurement items 17 to 20 October 2026. Wholesale: simple average of half-hourly Elexon APXMIDP prices with non-zero volume, 1 January 2024 to 17 October 2026; October is month-to-date. Gas cost of power (Figure 1): NBP p/therm × 10 ÷ 29.3071 = £/MWh of gas, ÷ 0.5 for a 50%-efficient CCGT (Ofgem's central spark-spread assumption) 13; gas only. October uses Purely Energy's day-ahead average to 16 October; Ofgem's series ends in June 2026. Gas storage: GIE's official EU figure for 1 October 17; mid-October UK figures disagree between sources, so we quote none. TEC Register: rows not marked Built, summed "MW Increase / Decrease". CfD prices: AR7 and AR8 in 2024 prices. AO10: rules and estimates as summarised in Signal Issue 1 (sources checked 17 October 2026). European offshore deals: announcements from 1 to 20 October 2026; the Danish award (4 August) is included for reference only.
