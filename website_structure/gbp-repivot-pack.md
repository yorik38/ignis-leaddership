## 1. Read this before you change anything

Three findings from the audit, in order of how much they matter.

**The freelancer's "backlinks within 20km" almost certainly did nothing.** I searched for your brand name, your phone number and your street address across the indexed web and found no directory citations, no local listings, and nothing carrying your NAP details. What is normally sold as "500 Google Maps citations within X km" is a batch of throwaway pages embedding map pins and driving-direction links pointing at your address. Google does not treat those as citations. They are not counted, and in volume they are the kind of footprint Google's spam systems ignore at best. The practical upshot is good news for you: there is very little to unwind, so a category change will not orphan a citation network, because there is not one.

**Your search index is still the old business.** Google still holds `/old-home`, `/coaching/1-1-transformation` and `/5-mental-fitness-techniques`, and still describes the site as executive coaching and hypnotherapy built on "The Clear Identity Method". Those pages are no longer in your repo and there are no redirects for them in `vercel.json`, so they are 404ing while ranked. This matters more than the map listing does. Fix is in section 9.

**Be realistic about the map pack.** Nobody searches "bid management consultant near me" the way they search "life coach near me". Local pack intent for this niche is close to zero, so the profile will not be a lead source in the way it might have been for coaching. It is still worth doing properly, for three reasons: it is what shows when someone Googles your name after a LinkedIn post or a pitch, it is a verified entity signal that feeds Google's knowledge graph and the AI answer engines you have already opened your robots.txt to, and right now it actively contradicts your website. Treat this as credibility hygiene, not lead generation. The real pipeline work is LinkedIn, the newsletter and the insights pages.

## 6. Address, service area and hours

**Hide the street address and convert to a service area business.** Right now 35 Houseman Cres is published on a public Google listing. That is your home. There is no client-facing reason for it to be visible, and the trade is favourable: you lose proximity-based pack ranking that was never going to convert for bid consulting anyway.

In Edit profile > Location, clear the address, then set the service area. Because you work nationwide, go broad rather than listing towns: add **United Kingdom** as the primary area, then **Greater Manchester** and **North West England** underneath it so the local relevance is still explicit. Google caps you at 20 service areas, so do not burn them on individual Cheshire towns when a country-level entry covers the same ground and matches how you actually sell.

Note that a national service area and a Manchester map pin pull in slightly different directions: the broader you go, the less proximity weighting you get. That trade is the right one here, because your buyers are not searching by distance.

Hours: set them rather than leaving "Add business hours" showing. Monday to Friday, 09:00 to 17:30 is fine. An incomplete hours field is one of the things dragging your profile strength ring down.

Attributes worth ticking if offered: "Online appointments", "Onsite services", "Identifies as LGBTQ+ owned" or similar only if true, and "Appointment required".

## 9. The website fix that matters more than any of this

Add these to the `redirects` array in `vercel.json`. They are ranked pages currently returning 404, which is wasted equity and a poor first impression for anyone arriving from a stale search result.

```json
{ "source": "/old-home", "destination": "/", "permanent": true },
{ "source": "/coaching/1-1-transformation", "destination": "/services", "permanent": true },
{ "source": "/coaching/:slug*", "destination": "/", "permanent": true },
{ "source": "/5-mental-fitness-techniques", "destination": "/insights", "permanent": true }
```

Then run the three old URLs through Search Console's URL Inspection tool and request indexing on the destinations. Worth checking Search Console for any other legacy coaching URLs still holding impressions before you finalise the list, since I could only see what public search would show me.

**And a bigger gap, while we are here.** Your website's services section shows only four offers, all of them the transformation half: Discovery, Consulting, Solution Development, Coaching and Training. The fractional bid executive work, which your LinkedIn leads with and which is the more immediate sale, does not appear anywhere on the site. That is why my first pass at these services missed it too. Someone who finds you through the website cannot buy the thing you are most obviously ready to sell. Worth a separate conversation about adding a fifth card, or restructuring the section around the two partner modes the way LinkedIn does.
