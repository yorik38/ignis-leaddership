## 4. Benchmark: Luk Smeyers' About vs yours

Luk Smeyers (The Visible Authority) runs his About as a five-question FAQ: why clients call, what they suffer from, who the ideal client is, what they get, how to start. Worth borrowing two things from it, and leaving the rest.

**Borrow:**
- **An explicit "who this is for" line.** Luk spells out his ideal client in a bulleted list. Yours implies it ("live strategic bid," "strategically important bids") but never states it as plainly. A one-line addition — something like *"This tends to fit energy, infrastructure and industrial organisations bidding complex, high-value tenders"* — would let the wrong-fit reader self-select out faster, and the right one recognise themselves faster.
- **An explicit next step.** Luk ends with exactly how to start working with him and a link. Yours ends on the website URL, which works, but doesn't tell the reader what happens if they click it. Not urgent to change, but worth knowing it's the one structural gap.

**Don't borrow:**
- **The "we" voice.** Luk writes as an institution ("clients call us," "we get contacted"), which suits a firm-like advisory brand built around a newsletter and an authority platform. Your site is built the other way — a named founder, a photo, a first-person quote ("I translate senior leadership challenge into execution..."). Switching to "we" would fight the personal-brand positioning you've already built, not reinforce it.
- **The FAQ scaffolding itself.** Five numbered Q&As read like a landing page, which fits Luk's more transactional "apply to work with us" model. Yours reads as one person talking directly to another, which fits a fractional/advisory relationship built on trust before a scope exists. The narrative paragraph structure is the right container for that; it just needed the two-line hook from section 2 above, not a structural overhaul.

**Bottom line:** your About doesn't need Luk's format. It needs what it now has — the same hook as the website — plus, optionally, one line naming who it's for.

## 5. The banner — worth changing

Short answer: yes. Your current banner text ("Bid more. Win more. Systematically." / "AI-enabled bid transformation for complex tenders") is the old hero line, not the new one, so it's now telling a different story to the About section directly beneath it.

What Luk's banner does well, worth copying: one bold statement, high contrast, legible at thumbnail size, nothing competing for attention. What it doesn't have that yours does: proof. Your bp / RWE / Bouygues logo row is a real asset Luk doesn't have an equivalent of (he substitutes a newsletter and an authority platform instead) — keep it.

**Recommended banner copy (revised):**
- Eyebrow (small, uppercase, teal accent): FRACTIONAL BID EXECUTIVE
- Headline (large, bold): Bid sparring partner. Practice transformation partner.
- Footer row: bp / RWE / Bouygues Energies & Services / Bouygues UK logos, unchanged from today
- Small corner mark: www.ignisleadership.com

*Why the rewrite:* Luk's banner headline is six words. My first draft ran to eleven. At banner scale (and especially at LinkedIn's thumbnail size, where the banner shows tiny) length is the enemy, so the line has been cut down to the same six-word economy: "Bid sparring partner. Practice transformation partner." Same two ideas, same order, no connecting words. Nothing else about the design changes.

Layout keeps the same skeleton as your current banner — logos bottom-left-of-centre, message upper-right, left ~350px clear for your profile photo, which LinkedIn overlaps regardless of what's underneath it.
