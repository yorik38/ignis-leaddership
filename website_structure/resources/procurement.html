Most procurement time is lost between the gates, not inside them.

Who this paper is for

CEOs and heads of procurement, commercial and development at renewable energy developers and utilities in the UK and Europe, onshore and offshore.

The Ignis view

The challenge is no longer building agents. It's building the system behind them. Seven gates, one record: from package strategy to financial close. Agents keep the next gate ready, so the project does not stop between them.

01 · Why now

The clocks run while documents wait

Short answer

Once a project wins its route to market, three clocks start at once: the support contract, the grid and the supply chain. None of them pauses for a tender pack sitting in someone's inbox.

18 months

After signing a UK CfD, the generator must show spend of at least 10% of pre-commissioning costs, or the required project commitments.

90 days

To accept a GB transmission connection offer once it arrives.

Up to 4 years

To secure large power transformers. Cables take two to three years, and lead times have almost doubled since 2021.

When costs outrun the project

In July 2023 Vattenfall stopped developing Norfolk Boreas in its current form, citing cost increases of up to 40%.

In May 2025 Ørsted stopped Hornsea 4 in its current form after winning a CfD in 2024. Its chief executive pointed to "the combination of increased supply chain costs, higher interest rates and increased execution risk".

Neither was a paperwork problem. Both show how little room there is between award and investment decision.

Procurement now feeds the bid

Under the EU Net-Zero Industry Act, the ability to deliver on time is a test to qualify for renewables auctions, and resilience can be scored. Supplier evidence is no longer only for lenders. It goes into the round itself.

Onshore and offshore alike: the packages differ in size and number. Both lose the same weeks between gates.

The Ignis view

Policy, grid, the supply chain and lenders each move on their own calendar, and projects stall in the gaps between them. The industry needs coordination from policy and regulation through to the industrial plan. Inside the developer the same rule applies: start procurement before the round is won, and keep every gate ready before the last one closes.

02 · Where procurement waits

Seven gates, and the pauses between them

Short answer

Drawn as a plan, procurement is a line of gates. In practice much of the elapsed time sits in the gaps, where work waits for a decision or is redone because something upstream changed.

What stays the same

The gates, the decisions and the people who take them. Shortlist, preferred bidder, risk allocation and signature stay with your team and your board.

What changes

The next pack is drafted while this gate is decided. Nothing is retyped. A change is approved once and reaches every document that uses it.

Find your own pauses in an afternoon

Take one package you closed last year. Put the date each gate opened and closed on one line. The gaps between them are your pauses. For the two longest, ask what the team was waiting for, and whether it had to wait.

02 · Early procurement

Procurement starts before the round is won

Short answer

Before award nothing is secured, and every hour spent on suppliers is development money at risk. Yet the bid needs supplier evidence: prices, lead times and the ability to deliver. Done once, on one record, early procurement feeds the bid and becomes the first tender pack.

1

Sound the market

A long list of suppliers per package, with capacity, lead times and delivery slots for the target year.

2

Indicative prices into the floor price

Current supplier prices and dates feed the capex and the floor price, not last year's numbers.

3

Evidence the round can score

Delivery, resilience and sustainability evidence gathered once, for the round and later for lenders.

4

A draft package strategy

Packages, interfaces and the contracting approach sketched, so gate 1 opens with a draft, not a blank page.

What agents carry

  • Supplier long lists and market notes, kept current.
  • Questions to suppliers logged, so none is asked twice before and after award.
  • Indicative prices and dates linked to the bid model.

What your people decide

  • How much to spend before award, project by project.
  • Which suppliers to engage, and on what terms.
  • The capex and dates the bid rests on.
The Ignis view

Early procurement often draws in people from technical, commercial and finance teams, and much of it is redone after award. Make it lighter and reusable, and the same development budget carries more projects to the round, with better evidence in each bid. Spend less to learn more before award, then start gate 1 from what you already know.

03 · Process first

First, look at what you run today

Short answer

The first step is not a tool. It is a clear look at how your team takes a package from strategy to signature, where it waits, and why.

1

Offers that are not the same offer

Each bidder prices a slightly different scope, so the cheapest offer is often the one that left most out.

FixRestate every offer onto one scope before anyone ranks it.
2

Questions asked and answered twice

Clarifications sit in email threads, and the same question comes back from each bidder.

FixOne clarification log, answered once and shared.
3

A model that is not the contract

Price, indexation and caps move in negotiation; the financial model catches up later.

FixModel and draft read the same terms, and a change needs an approval.
4

Dates that do not agree

The grid date, the CfD milestone and the supplier's programme live in three places.

FixOne date register with one owner, checked at every gate.
5

The lender pack built at the end

Due diligence starts from a blank folder, months after the evidence was first gathered.

FixBuild the pack as you go, from the record.

Start with two. Pick the two pauses that cost you most, and find the reason for each. Fix those first. The rest can wait.

What do you run today? Map one package from strategy to signature. Remove steps that add nothing. Simplify what is left.

What does it run on? One record per project: scope, offers, terms, dates and evidence, versioned and owned.

Then add the agents, on the redesigned process, with named people deciding at each gate.

Revisit the real problem first

Why does a tender take as long as it does? Which steps exist because a lender or a rule needs them, and which because "we have always done it"? Is the package split right for this market? Automate a broken process and you get a faster broken process.

04 · Comparing suppliers

Compare like for like before you rank

Short answer

Ranking offers that answer different scopes is really ranking scopes. Restate every offer onto one grid first, and the real differences show up in days rather than in the final week.

What agents carry

  • Read every offer and map it onto the grid.
  • Flag gaps, exclusions and dates that miss the grid.
  • Draft clarifications and log each answer once.
  • Restate prices onto one index and one scope.

What your people decide

  • Which gaps matter, and what they are worth.
  • The shortlist and the preferred bidder.
  • Where to push and where to concede.
  • Supplier relationships and the final call.

Onshore and offshore: offshore runs fewer, larger packages with long supplier lists for each; onshore runs more, smaller packages, often several projects at once. The grid works for both, and the record keeps every past offer for the next tender.

04 · Contract and lender pack

Contract and lender pack, from one record

Short answer

The comparison, the contract, the model and the lender pack describe the same project. When they read from one record, a change made in negotiation reaches all of them, with a name and a date on it.

Approve changes, do not email them. A price, date or risk moves only with a named approval on the record. The consistency check runs before every gate.

Lenders ask the same questions. Keep every question and answer from the last close, and the next data room starts half built.

05 · Same team, more output

The same team, more output, better quality

Short answer

The aim is not fewer people. It is more packages, bidders and questions handled well by the people you have, with fewer errors reaching a lender.

Quantity

  • More packages run in parallel.
  • More bidders assessed properly, not cut early for lack of time.
  • The next gate ready on the day this one closes.

Quality

  • Every number traced to its source.
  • Model, contract and pack in step.
  • Terms won and conceded kept for the next project.
06 · Inside the system

A governed system on your data, better with every project

Short answer

Ignis builds the system around your team, on your own documents and in your own environment. It keeps the record, runs the repeatable work and brings every decision to a named person.

The AI manager

Breaks a goal such as "turbine supply to preferred bidder" into tasks, gives each to the right agent, checks the output and stops at each gate for a decision.

The record is yours

Offers, terms, dates and lender answers stay in your environment under your access rules. Each project leaves it better for the next.

What stays human

The shortlist, the preferred bidder, risk allocation, the walk-away and the signature. Supplier and lender relationships.

Supplier prices stay confidential, and you can prove it

Offers, prices and terms stay in your environment. You decide where the models run and what they can read, and every read, change and approval leaves an audit trail you can show suppliers, partners and lenders.

Buy a platform, or build your own

Platforms are quick to start and suit standard tasks. Supplier terms and lessons are your edge, so we help you build your own: a governed system on your data, handed over to your team.

Each project leaves the record richer: better terms to start from, fewer questions to answer, faster gates next time.

08 · Where to start

Start with your two biggest pauses

Short answer

Not with a platform. First comes a short, low-risk discovery and gap review, then a map of one package from strategy to signature: two weeks at most. The price stays open until we have seen the work.

The first weeks
Week 1Discovery and gap review

Your goals, your package plan, and the two biggest pauses in procurement, with their causes.

Week 2Process mapping

One recent package traced end to end on its own documents. What people keep, what agents carry, where the gates sit.

Weeks 3 to 5Sprint

Redesign the process, build the comparison grid and the record, and set the gates for the next live package.

By day 90Pilot

Your team runs a live tender on the new way of working, then embeds it.
