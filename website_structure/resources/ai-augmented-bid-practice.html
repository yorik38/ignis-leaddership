Practical guide

How to build an AI-augmented bid practice

An AI-augmented bid practice combines governed AI workflows with named human decision rights across capture, pursuit and handover. This practical AI bid management guide helps you assess readiness, redesign one workflow and choose a controlled first test.

Read the guide ↓Download the guide ↗
Yorik TisseauBid transformation adviser · Prepared for review 9 September 2026
Start with the workflow

Do not start with a list of AI tasks.

A bid is not a document-production exercise. It is a sequence of commercial, technical and delivery decisions, made under time pressure and with imperfect information.

The useful question is not simply where AI could save time. It is where a bounded agent contribution could improve the quality or timing of a decision while a named person remains accountable.

The central reframeDesign the decision, context, controls and human gate before choosing the agent.
How to use it

Assess one real workflow.

Use a live, recent or representative pursuit. Avoid rating the organisation in the abstract.

VisibleClear, named and evidenced in the workflow.
PartialPresent in places, but inconsistent or dependent on individuals.
UnclearNot yet defined, tested or owned.
The 12 questions

Look for the conditions that make a test credible.

There is no single maturity score. The pattern across these six areas matters more than the total. Mark each statement Visible, Partial or Unclear, then use the prompt to challenge the workflow.

01

Business outcome

  1. The workflow begins with a named business or bid decision, not a general ambition to use AI or save time.
  2. The accountable people agree what a useful output must contain and what would make it unsafe or misleading.

Challenge promptWhich consequential decision should this work make easier to reach?

02

Trusted context

  1. The required tender, customer, commercial, technical and organisational sources are identified, current and available.
  2. The workflow distinguishes approved evidence from stale, unverified, confidential or other-customer material.

Challenge promptCould a reviewer trace every important output to an authorised source?

03

Agents, tools and roles

  1. The agent contribution is bounded and specific: retrieving, comparing, checking, structuring or routing work.
  2. A named person is responsible for directing the work, resolving exceptions and deciding whether the output is used.

Challenge promptIs the agent's job clear enough without saying “help with the bid”?

04

Evaluation and refinement

  1. The team can test accuracy, completeness, source use and omissions against a human-reviewed example or baseline.
  2. The workflow defines what happens when evidence is uncertain, sources conflict or the agent fails.

Challenge promptHow will you know it is wrong before it affects the pursuit?

05

Human decision gates

  1. Named people retain authority for qualification, strategy, commitments, risk acceptance and submission.
  2. Each gate states what evidence is required, who approves and how the decision is recorded.

Challenge promptWho can say yes, no or not yet?

06

Measurement and learning

  1. The test measures more than speed: rework, waiting, omissions, corrections and whether decisions happen earlier.
  2. Approved learning can improve the next pursuit without allowing prior answers or assumptions to become truth.

Challenge promptWhat could this bid teach the system that the next team can use safely?

Read the pattern

Readiness is not a total score.

Your strongest first use case sits where three conditions intersect. A gap in one of them is not failure. It tells you what to resolve before you spend more.

  1. 01
    The work recurs and matters. It creates a meaningful commercial, risk, capacity or quality consequence.
  2. 02
    The context can be trusted and evaluated. The right evidence is available and a knowledgeable person can judge the output.
  3. 03
    The test can be controlled. Historical, synthetic or otherwise safe material allows you to learn before live delivery depends on it.
Choose the first workflow

Make the use case testable.

Turn the opportunity into one sentence before discussing tools or platforms.

When [named user] needs to [job or decision], the workflow will [bounded agent contribution] using [authorised context], before [named person] decides [human gate].
Recurring

Does this happen often enough to learn from?

Consequential

Would improvement matter to growth, risk or capacity?

Grounded

Are the inputs available, authorised and current?

Evaluable

Can a knowledgeable person judge success?

Controllable

Can it be tested safely before live reliance?

30-day test canvas

Define the experiment before building it.

Complete these six fields with the people who own the work, the evidence and the decision. Keep the first test deliberately narrow.

01 · User and job

Who needs what decision?

Name the user, the task or decision, the current friction and its practical consequence.

02 · Agent boundary

What may the agent do?

State the preparation or coordination work it may perform, and what it must not do.

03 · Trusted context

What may it use?

List the authorised sources, owners, access conditions and rules for stale or conflicting information.

04 · Human gate

Who remains accountable?

Name the person who reviews the evidence, resolves exceptions and decides whether the work advances.

05 · Evaluation

How will you detect failure?

Set the baseline, checks, tolerances, known failure modes and escalation route.

06 · Test and decision

What will 30 days prove?

Choose controlled material, reviewers and measures, then define the decision to stop, refine or expand.
