How to build an AI-augmented bid practice

Start with one useful agent. Build trust before you scale.

Explore the framework ↓
Why now

Start with the pressure your team already feels.

Your bid team does not need another AI initiative added to an already full workload. It needs relief from the searching, chasing and rework that steal time from judgement.

I use the six parts below to help you choose one real problem, build a governed workflow around it and learn before you scale. You can start without pretending the whole practice is ready.

The principleStart with one useful agent. Earn the right to connect the next.
The route

A safer way to start.

Build confidence in stages, with your people involved from the beginning.

Make it trustworthySet the context, tests, authority and recovery path before relying on it.
Connect what earns trustAdd specialist roles only when the handover creates measurable value.
Six-part architecture

Build a practice your people can trust.

Each part answers a question your bid team already faces. Together, they turn an isolated AI tool into a workflow that people can understand, govern and improve.

01 · Business outcome

Start with the bid decision you need to improve.

You do not need another AI experiment looking for a use. Start where your team feels the cost now: a late qualification call, weak evidence, repeated rework or senior time lost to coordination.

I help you name the decision, the person who owns it and what better must mean in practice. That gives the agent a job to serve and gives you a reason to stop if the work is not creating value.

  • Decision and owner
  • Baseline and acceptance criteria
  • Clear stop condition

Ask What must improve, and what would make us stop?

02 · Trusted context

Give the agent the context your people trust.

Your team already knows which sources are current, which past bids are useful and which answers should never be reused. An agent does not know that unless you make the rules explicit.

Together, we map approved sources, access rights, ownership and precedence when evidence conflicts. The result is not just better retrieval. It is work your reviewers can trace and challenge.

  • Approved sources
  • Access and ownership
  • Evidence traceability

Ask What may the system trust, access and reuse?

03 · Agents and tools

Give every agent one job it can own.

A useful agent is not a digital Bid Director. It has one bounded role: compare requirements, retrieve evidence, prepare a compliance view or coordinate a handover.

I help you define its inputs, tools, output and limits, including the point where it must return control to a person. If a fixed rule is safer, we use one. The goal is dependable work, not technical theatre.

  • One bounded role
  • Minimum necessary tools
  • Explicit handback

Ask Why an agent, and when must it hand back?

04 · Evaluate and refine

Find the failure before your bid team does.

A polished answer can still be incomplete, unsupported or wrong. Before anyone relies on the workflow, you need to see how it behaves with ordinary cases, edge cases, conflicting evidence and poor inputs.

We compare it with the way the work happens now, capture the corrections and separate model failure from weak process or missing context. That is how confidence is earned, not assumed.

  • Representative test cases
  • Quality and exception checks
  • Recorded corrections

Ask How will we know it is wrong before work advances?

05 · Human decision gates

Keep your judgement where the stakes are highest.

Your people should not become passive reviewers of machine output. They should stay in control of qualification, strategy, commitments, risk and submission.

I help you make those decision gates visible: who decides, what evidence they need, when the work pauses and how the team recovers when conditions change. The system prepares the decision. Your accountable leader makes it.

  • Named decision owner
  • Evidence threshold
  • Pause and recovery route

Ask Who can say yes, no or not yet?

06 · Measure and learn

Make every pilot earn its next step.

Speed alone is not success if the team spends the saved time correcting errors. Track quality, rework, review burden, cycle time and exceptions, then ask whether the workflow changed a decision or released useful capacity.

I help you turn repeated corrections into stronger sources, roles, tests and policies. You then choose deliberately: stop, refine, repeat, connect or scale.

  • Quality and rework
  • Capacity and cycle time
  • Deliberate scale decision

Ask Has this workflow earned more scope?

The first pilot

Choose a Goldilocks use case.

The best first pilot is neither trivial nor transformational. It is repeatable enough to structure, important enough to matter and bounded enough to evaluate. Turn it into one sentence before discussing tools or platforms.

When [named user] needs to [job or decision], the workflow will [bounded agent contribution] using [authorised context], before [named person] decides [human gate].
Recurring

Does this happen often enough to learn from?

Consequential

Would improvement matter to growth, risk or capacity?

Grounded

Are the inputs available, authorised and current?

Evaluable

Can a knowledgeable person judge success?

Controllable

Can it be tested safely before live reliance?

Build progressively

Six controlled moves.

Prove one role, strengthen the foundations and add coordination only when it solves a real constraint.

01 · Choose

One workflow

Name the bottleneck, outcome, owner, baseline and non-negotiable controls.

02 · Prepare

A trusted workspace

Assemble approved sources, permissions, rules, expected outputs and representative cases.

03 · Build

One specialist

Give the agent one bounded role and the minimum tools it needs. Keep the first handover to a person.

04 · Test

Shadow mode

Run beside the existing process. Capture accuracy, completeness, exceptions, review effort and recovery.

05 · Connect

A second role

Add another specialist only when the handover between roles creates measurable value.

06 · Orchestrate

The workflow

Coordinate roles when sequencing, shared state and routing become the constraint.

Apply it to your practice

Map the first credible pilot.

Bring one workflow and the business decision it must improve. We can identify the operating-model gap, the smallest useful agent role and the controls required before the work advances.
