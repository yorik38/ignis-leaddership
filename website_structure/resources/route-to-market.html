At a glance

Choose the route before the round chooses it for you.

Five messages for your leadership team.

1

Every bid is development money at risk.

Until a project wins, what you spend on it may never come back. A cheaper bid lets the same budget back more projects.

2

The route is a decision, not a deadline.

Pick the anchor route for each project before the next round or buyer process picks it for you. All four routes draw on the same facts.

3

Know how the round pays.

In a pay-as-clear round your floor price decides whether you win. In a pay-as-bid round your bid is your price.

4

Process first, data second, agents third.

Look at how your team chooses and bids today, fix the two biggest pain points, structure the evidence, then add agents.

5

Build your own system, and own it.

You can buy a platform, or build your own. We help you build your own, on your data: people choose and sign, agents carry the load.

Who this paper is for

CEOs and heads of development, commercial and origination at renewables developers, IPPs and utilities in the UK and Europe, onshore and offshore.

Offshore seabed leasing rounds are a separate topic and a separate paper.

The Ignis view

The challenge is no longer building agents. It's building the system behind them.

01 · Why now

More rounds, more routes, the same team

Short answer

Support rounds are bigger and more contested, the rules are changing across Europe, and buyers are pickier. Each project now has more ways to reach market, and each way asks for its own case.

14.7 GW

Awarded across 201 projects in the UK's seventh CfD round, the largest round to date.

2 times

Germany's August 2026 onshore wind round drew 5,250 MW of bids for 2,520 MW on offer.

3 times

France's latest onshore wind tender drew 2,378 MW for 800 MW, and the average price fell by €9.5/MWh.

The rules are moving

Across the EU, at least 30% of each country's auctioned renewable volume, or 6 GW a year, must now carry non-price criteria. Responsible business conduct, cybersecurity and the ability to deliver on time are tests to qualify. Resilience and sustainability can also be scored.

From 17 July 2027, new direct price support for wind and solar in the EU has to take the form of two-way contracts for difference or an equivalent. In the UK, CfDs for wind and solar now run for 20 years from AR7.

Buyers are pickier

Disclosed European PPAs came to 13.1 GW across 247 deals in 2025, down from 15.3 GW in 2024. Spain stayed the largest market at 3.9 GW.

Spain shows why one route is never enough. Its last renewables auction, in November 2022, offered 3,300 MW and awarded 45.5 MW. Projects there went to market through PPAs instead.

The UK clock: AR8 applications closed on 7 August 2026. The budget and auction notices are due on 19 November, with results expected in February 2027.

The Ignis view

Policy, grid, supply chain and buyers each set their own calendar. Projects stall in the gaps between them. The industry needs coordination from policy and regulation through to the industrial plan, so projects move through each gate without pauses or drop-outs. A developer cannot fix that alone, but it can make its own route decision early, on one set of facts, and stop rebuilding the case for every counterparty.

02 · The four routes

Four ways to sell the power, four different cases to make

Short answer

Every route answers one question: who pays for the power, for how long and on what terms. What changes is who you must convince, and the evidence they want.

1

CfD round or state auction

A government round awards long-term price support. The UK runs CfD allocation rounds such as AR7 and AR8; France runs CRE tenders; Germany, the Netherlands and Spain run their own auctions.

You convince: the round's rules, with a sealed price and the evidence to qualify.

2

Merchant

The project sells at market prices, often with hedges, short-term deals or a trading partner. Common as the tail after a contract ends, and sometimes as the plan for part of the output.

You convince: your board and lenders that the price case holds.

3

Corporate or private PPA

A long-term contract with a buyer: a company, a utility or a trader. It can be physical, sleeved through a supplier, or virtual (financial). Terms, tenor and credit decide its value.

You convince: one buyer's energy, legal and credit teams.

4

Private wire

A direct cable from the project to a nearby user, such as an industrial site or a data centre, usually alongside a grid connection. Energy down the wire avoids most network charges, but it needs its own consent, land rights and licence position.

You convince: one user, the landowners on the route and the planners.

02 · How the routes compare

No route wins on every count

Short answer

The trade-off is always the same: certainty against upside and control. Read across a row to see what you give up for what you gain.

Know how the round pays

The UK CfD is pay-as-clear: every successful project in a pot and year gets the same clearing price. Bidding lower raises your chance of winning, not your income, so the work that matters is an honest floor price.

Germany's onshore wind auctions are pay-as-bid: each winner gets its own bid. There the bid price is the income, and every euro counts twice.

Most projects combine routes

A CfD covers the contract years and leaves a merchant tail. A PPA can cover part of the output while the rest sells at market. The useful question is which route is the anchor: the one that makes the project financeable.

02 · Choosing the anchor

Four questions find the anchor route

Short answer

Ask them per project, at least once a year and whenever a round, a buyer or the grid date changes. The answer is the anchor; other routes can sit alongside it.

Offshore

Scale and capital cost usually make a state round the anchor, with corporate PPAs part of the mix in some markets. The grid date and the supply chain set which delivery year is credible. With fewer, larger rounds, the value of agents offshore is cost and control, not volume: a lower cost per bid and tighter control of every number and claim.

Onshore

All four routes are live. A strong corporate buyer or a nearby large user can beat the next round on timing, and a round can beat a thin PPA market on price certainty.

Many markets, the same questions

The names change: CfD allocation rounds in the UK, CRE tenders in France, EEG auctions in Germany, renewables auctions in Spain and the Netherlands. The questions do not: can the grid date meet the delivery year, does the floor price clear, who else would buy the power, and who can carry the price risk.

Where teams slip: answering the first question from the round calendar instead of the grid date, and the second from last year's capex.

03 · Process first

First, look at how you decide today

Short answer

Most of the value lost in route and bid work is lost before and between documents, not inside them. So the first step is not a tool. It is a clear look at the process you run today, its bottlenecks and the reasons behind them.

1

The deadline picks the route

The next window decides where the project goes, not a comparison of routes.

FixA portfolio calendar and an anchor route per project, reviewed each quarter.
2

Evidence rebuilt every time

Land, consent, grid, yield and cost evidence is gathered again for each round or buyer.

FixOne evidence library per project, with an owner and a date on every item.
3

The floor price set late

The walk-away is agreed in the final week, on inputs nobody has rechecked.

FixSet the floor at the bid or no-bid gate, on current capex, yield and grid evidence.
4

The same project, told differently

Capacity, dates and prices drift between the round, a PPA offer and the board paper.

FixCross-checks as a step no submission can skip.
5

Lessons never written down

Results, clearing prices and buyer feedback stay in inboxes.

FixA short debrief that goes back into the record after every round.

Start with two. Pick the two loss points that cost you most, and find the reason for each. Fix those first. The rest can wait.

What do you run today? Map how the route is chosen and how a bid is built. Remove steps that add nothing. Simplify what is left.

What does it run on? One record per project: facts, evidence and assumptions, versioned and owned.

Then add the agents, on the redesigned process, with named people deciding at each gate.

Revisit the real problem first

Why does a bid take as long as it does? Which steps exist because of a rule, and which because "we have always done it"? Is this the right round for this project at all? Automate a broken process and you get a faster broken process.

04 · Winning the round

Eight steps to win the round

Short answer

The same steps work for a CfD application, a CRE tender, a PPA process or a private wire offer. Agents prepare; people decide at every step.

Value a bid before you write it

What agents change

  • Onshore: more rounds and buyers covered by the same team. Offshore: a lower cost per bid and more control, not more bids.
  • Senior time moves from chasing and reconciling to price, partners and negotiation.
  • Every claim traced to a source, every number matched.
  • Each round leaves a better record for the next.
05 · Inside the system

A governed system on your data, learning every round

Short answer

Ignis builds the system around your team, on your own documents and in your own environment. It keeps the record, runs the repeatable work and brings every decision to a named person.

The AI manager

Breaks a goal such as "AR8 application for project B" into tasks, gives each to the right agent, checks the output and stops at each gate for a decision.

The record is yours

Facts, evidence, past bids and buyer terms stay in your environment under your access rules. The more rounds you run, the better it gets.

What stays human

The route, the price, the walk-away, partner terms and the signature. Relationships with buyers, communities and government.

People keep the decisions. Agents carry the rules, the evidence and the first drafts.

05 · Build your own

You can buy a platform, or build your own. We help you build your own.

Short answer

Platforms are quick to switch on and work well for tasks that are the same in every company. Route choice and bids are where your edge sits: your projects, your evidence, your prices and your lessons. That part is worth owning.

Buy a platform

Fast to start, with a ready-made interface and support. A good fit for standard tasks shared across the industry. The vendor sets the data model and the roadmap; your process adapts to the tool.

Build your own, with Ignis

Designed around your process, after a discovery of how you work today. A governed system on your own data, in your environment, under your access rules. Your team owns the system and the know-how, and changes it as rounds change.

Many teams will use both: a platform for standard work, their own system for the work that wins. Your own system can still run on off-the-shelf models and tools. What matters is that the process, the record and the gates are yours. Either way, fix the process before you add the tool.

Getting adoption right

The technology is rarely the hard part. Four things decide whether the system is used.

1

Confidentiality you can prove

Agree where the models run, what they can read and who can see what, before any agent starts. Every read and change leaves an audit trail you can show your board, partners and lenders.

2

Roles, not redundancies

The aim is the same team doing more and better work. Each role is redesigned with the people in it: what they keep, what agents carry, and who runs the AI manager day to day.

3

An executive sponsor

A change in how routes are chosen and bids are built needs backing from the top. A named executive owns the outcome from discovery onwards and clears the blockers.

4

Capability handover

Ignis advises, designs and builds alongside your team, then hands over. By the end, your people run, improve and extend the system themselves.

Your system, your data, your team's know-how. Ignis helps you build it and hands it over.

06 · Self-check

How ready is your team for the next round?

Short answer

Ten yes or no questions. Tick what is true today, not what is planned.

0/10 Tick what is true today
0 to 3 Start with the route decision and a bid or no-bid gate
4 to 7 Fix the evidence library and the cross-checks, then add agents
8 to 10 Ready to take on more rounds and buyers with the same team

Questions for your team on Monday

  1. Which rounds and buyer deadlines fall in the next 90 days, and who owns each one?
  2. What is our floor price for each, and when was it last tested?
  3. Where does the same project appear with different numbers?
  4. What did we learn from the last round, and where is it written down?
