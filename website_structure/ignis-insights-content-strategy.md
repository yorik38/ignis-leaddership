## 1. Where Insights actually is

Six newsletter issues exist. Two are published, four are approved and sitting in
`04_Ready/Long-Form/`. Three short-form companions are drafted and awaiting review. The
LinkedIn cadence defines four slots a week and, in practice, only the Monday slot is
running.

| Issue | Title | Primary reader | State |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | Bids are lost before the writing starts | Sponsor-leaning | Published 24 Aug |
| 02 | Why does every bid feel like starting again? | Both | Published 31 Aug |
| 03 | Not every criterion deserves equal effort | Champion | Ready |
| 04 | Late reviews cannot change weak bids | Champion | Ready |
| 05 | Your bid manager is expensive middleware | Sponsor | Ready |
| 06 | Faster bids are not better decisions | Sponsor | Ready |

The writing quality is high and the assurance discipline behind it is unusually strong.
The change histories are more rigorous than most agencies manage. That is not the issue.

The issue is that Insights is currently operating as **a publication** when the business
needs it to operate as **a demand-creation and qualification asset**. Those are different
jobs and they produce different pages.

## 2. What is misaligned, specifically

### 2.1 The hub advertises two titles that were rejected during assurance

This is live right now and is the most concrete finding in this document.

| Slot | What `insights.html` tells visitors is coming | What the approved Ready file actually is |
|---|---|---|
| Issue 03 | "We ran out of time on the question that mattered most." | "Not every criterion deserves equal effort" |
| Issue 04 | "We called it a red-team review. It was already too late." | "Late reviews cannot change weak bids" |

Both site titles are the pre-revision versions. They were replaced on 31 August under
`System/content-quality-standard.md`, which requires a title to explain the concept before
it intrigues, and both of the site's versions are exactly the "dramatic sentence whose
concept appears only later" pattern that standard names as weak. The site is publicly
committed to two titles the system has already rejected.

This is not just a tidying item. It shows there is no defined step that syncs Ready state
to the website, so the hub drifts silently. Section 7 proposes the fix.

### 2.2 The hub's H1 is the homepage slogan, so Insights has no promise of its own

`insights.html` opens with "Bid more. Win more. Systematically.", the same line as the
homepage H1 and the footer. The actual promise, "Practical field notes for stronger bid
teams", is demoted to a subheadline.

A reader arriving from LinkedIn or search has already been told what Ignis does. What
they need from this page is a reason to spend time here and a way to find the piece that
matters to them. Repeating the slogan gives them neither. This is finding W-18 in the
website audit and it resolves the same way: the slogan is a publication name, so it
belongs on the masthead, not as an H1.

### 2.3 Insights argues route two while the site is being repositioned to sell route one

Four of the six issues are practice-level: knowledge reuse, coordination cost, the bid
manager as middleware, speed versus judgement. Two are pursuit-level: criterion weighting
and late reviews.

The positioning brief says the opposite is the commercial reality. "Organisations rarely
fund a stand-alone 12 to 18 month transformation programme, but they will fund help with
a pursuit that is already causing pain." Practice transformation is meant to be built on
top of a live pursuit, not sold ahead of it.

So the content library is currently strongest at arguing the thing that is hardest to
sell, and thinnest at arguing the thing that opens the door. Issues 03 and 04 are the two
pieces closest to the entry point, and both are still in Ready.

### 2.4 The site's mechanism appears in no article, and the articles' thesis appears nowhere useful on the site

MAP is the site's headline framework. It is taught in no Insights piece. The five-level
maturity ladder is on the site and taught in no Insights piece. Conversely, the thesis
that expertise resets on every bid is now the site's hero subheadline and it came
directly from the content.

Content is feeding the site. The site is not feeding content. A reader who arrives from
an article has no way to recognise that the framework on the homepage is the same idea
they just read about, and a reader who arrives at the homepage has no article that shows
the framework working.

### 2.5 The article page has no route to a service

The current end-of-article furniture is a reader-engagement prompt that opens a mailto, a
newsletter signup, an author bio, and a link to the previous edition. There is no path to
the contact form, no path to either service route, and no relevance signal at all.

The mailto is worth keeping. It is warm, it is low-friction and it suits the tone. But a
Commercial Director who has just read "Your bid manager is expensive middleware" and
recognised their own organisation currently has nowhere to go except an email client.

### 2.6 The Brand OS finding is unresolved three issues later

The August audit scored the content 8.4 out of 10 for the Bid Director and 3.6 for the
economic buyer, and proposed the benefit ladder plus a "D" post type as the fix.

Progress since: issues 05 and 06 are sponsor-primary, which is real movement, and the
rung-3 bridge was applied to newsletter 01. But the titles are still process language
("bid manager", "bids", "criterion", "reviews"), no piece has ever named a sector, no
piece carries an economic consequence in its standfirst, and the D post type has not been
created. The Pattern and Proof Bank, which was the mechanism for making rung-3 claims
honestly sustainable, has not been started.

### 2.7 No piece names a sector

Not one of the six titles or standfirsts names an industry. Offshore wind appears inside
the bodies of issues 01 and 03, as story material rather than as an address to a reader.

This is the single cheapest ICP fix available anywhere in the system. A standfirst that
says "on an offshore wind bid" or "for a data centre pursuit" converts a general piece
about bidding into a piece that a specific reader recognises as being about their world,
at a cost of four words and zero loss of applicability to anyone else.

## 3. What Insights is for

Proposed statement of job, to be added to `ignis-linkedin-content-strategy.md` if Yorik
approves it:

> Insights exists to make the ICP recognise the root problem before Ignis is in the room,
> and to give the person who funds the bid function a reason to act on it. It is not a
> publication about bidding. It is the argument that the way their organisation bids is
> costing them something, made in public, repeatedly, with the practitioner credibility
> that makes it hard to dismiss.

Three consequences follow.

**Insights qualifies as well as attracts.** A piece that makes the wrong reader nod is
not a success. The measure is whether the right reader recognises themselves.

**Every piece belongs to a route.** Either it argues the live pursuit, which leads to
route one, or it argues the practice behind it, which leads to route two. A piece that
belongs to neither is an essay about bidding, and there is no shortage of those.

**Insights is where the proof lives before there is client proof.** Until the Pattern and
Proof Bank produces sourced outcomes, the honest proof available is method: how Yorik
thinks, how he designs a test, what he refuses to claim. Post 17, "Search is not enough
for bid reuse", is a good example of this and there is currently nothing like it in the
long-form library.

## 4. Reader and route architecture

### 4.1 Target mix per batch of six

Currently the library is roughly 4:2 practice to pursuit, and 3:3 champion to sponsor if
you count generously.

Proposed target across any batch of six, to be balanced over the batch rather than
assigned by weekday, consistent with the existing rule in `CONTENT-CONFIG.md`:

| Job | Count per six | Route | Primary reader |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pursuit diagnostic | 2 | One | Champion, sponsor secondary |
| Practice diagnostic | 2 | Two | Sponsor, champion secondary |
| Sector-anchored piece | 1 | Either | Both, ICP-specific |
| Method or proof piece | 1 | Two | Both, technical gatekeeper secondary |

That shifts pursuit content from two in six to at least two guaranteed plus whichever of
the sector piece lands there, and it introduces two article types the library does not
currently have.

### 4.2 The two new types

**Sector-anchored piece.** A diagnostic set explicitly inside one of the ICP's worlds.
Not a case study and not a client story, which are not available. A piece where the
tender type, the evaluation shape and the technical pressure are specific to offshore
wind, or data centres, or hospital MEP, and where the general lesson emerges from that
specificity rather than being illustrated by it.

Why it matters: it is the only article type that makes an ICP reader think "this person
has actually bid what I bid." That is a different and stronger reaction than "this person
understands bidding."

**Method or proof piece.** How Yorik designs a test, what he would measure, what would
make him abandon a use case. Post 17 does this in short form. Long form can go further:
show the artefact, show the evaluation criteria, show the failure mode. This is the piece
that answers the technical gatekeeper and it is the piece that makes the AI claims
credible without claiming an outcome.

Both types must obey the existing maturity rule. Design and hypothesis stay labelled as
design and hypothesis.

### 4.3 Sector anchoring rule

Proposed addition to the quality gate:

> At least one piece in three names a sector in the title or standfirst, drawn only from
> the sectors already stated in the site's About section: offshore wind, energy from
> waste, energy centres, data centres and hospital MEP. The integrated port energy system
> in newsletter 01 may be used as a story, since it is Yorik's own, but not as a claim to
> serve a port or grid sector. No sector is named that is not on this list.

### 4.4 Titles

The content quality standard is working and should not change. One addition, drawn from
the format implication in the positioning brief's section 7:

> Where a title names a problem bluntly, the body should resolve it fast with something
> small and concrete, a test, a rule or a short framework, rather than building the case
> across several sections before handing over the payoff.

Issue 03 is the live example: the title is in the right register, the body reaches the
three-question test only after several sections of argument. The fix is not less rigour.
It is showing less of the working before the reader gets something they can use.

## 5. Conversion architecture

### 5.1 The hub page — Agreed

**Live now**

> **H1:** Bid more. Win more. Systematically.
> **Sub:** Practical field notes for stronger bid teams.
> **Intro:** Practical field notes on the decisions, workflows and knowledge systems that help teams bid more and win more.

The sub and the intro say the same thing twice, and the H1 says nothing about Insights.

**Agreed copy**

> **Masthead:** Bid more. Win more. · The Ignis newsletter
> **H1:** Field notes from inside complex tenders
> **Sub:** What actually decides a pursuit, what it costs when the thinking arrives late, and what changes when a bid practice stops starting from zero.
> **Intro:** Written for the people who run high-stakes bids in energy, infrastructure and industrial markets, and for the people accountable for whether those bids win.

The intro names both readers and the ICP in one sentence, which is a job no page on the
site currently does.

### 5.2 Grouping by problem, shown as a tag rather than a section split — Agreed

The hub currently lists "Latest edition", "Past editions" and "Upcoming issues". Issue
number is the publisher's organising principle, not the reader's. A visitor who has just
recognised a problem wants the piece about that problem.

The grouping itself is agreed; splitting the hub into three separate sections is not.
Instead, each issue keeps its place in the existing list and carries a tag, in the site's
dark blue, naming its group. That makes the route architecture visible without
restructuring the page:

> **The pursuit in front of you** · issues about the decisions inside a live bid
> **The practice behind it** · issues about why every bid starts again
> **How I build and test this** · method, design and what I refuse to claim yet

Issues 03 and 04 carry the first tag. Issues 02, 05 and 06 carry the second. Issue 01
carries the first tag too, because its subject is sequencing inside a pursuit, even
though it touches both.

Drop "Upcoming issues" as a public feature, or keep it and add it to the publication
checklist in section 7. Advertising future titles publicly is only worth the maintenance
cost if it drives subscriptions, and there is no data either way. The safer version is a
single line: "A new issue most Mondays."

### 5.3 The article page — Agreed

Keep the mailto prompt, the author bio and the subscribe box. They are working and the
tone is right.

Add one contextual next step, matched to the article's route, between the reader prompt
and the subscribe box:

> **For a route-one article:** Reading this because of a bid that is live right now?
> That is usually the right moment to bring in an outside view. [Discuss a live bid →]
>
> **For a route-two article:** If this is a pattern rather than a bad week, the place to
> start is one bounded problem, not a programme. [Discuss your bid practice →]

Both lines are qualifying rather than selling, which suits the voice and suits a reader
who has just been given something useful for free. Apply whichever line matches the
piece's route, as required, so every article carries exactly one of the two.

Also add: a "related" link to the other article in the same group rather than only the
previous issue by number, and the sector tag where the piece has one.

### 5.4 The link between site and content, in both directions

Two changes close the gap identified in 2.4.

**Site to content.** Each of the two service routes on the homepage gets one line
pointing to the article that argues it. Not a list, one piece each, changed when a
stronger one exists. This makes the Insights library evidence for the service description
rather than a separate wing of the site.

**Content to site.** MAP needs one article that teaches it, and it should be the method
piece in the next batch. Not "here is my framework", which is brochure content, but the
argument for why scaling before evidence fails, with Map, Apply and Progress emerging as
the answer. That is the piece that makes the homepage framework mean something to a
reader who has met it in prose first.

## 6. Realignment plan for what already exists

Rules applied. Published pieces are the verbatim record and are never edited
retrospectively, per gate 8 in `CONTENT-CONFIG.md`. Ready pieces change only through a
new approval decision, per the state and gates rules in `AGENTS.md`. So "revise" below
always means "propose a revision for Yorik's approval", never "change it".

| Item | State | Recommendation | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| Issue 01, Bids are lost before the writing starts | Published | Keep as is. Tag it into the pursuit group on the hub | It is the best route-one argument in the library and it is currently filed only by issue number |
| Issue 02, Why does every bid feel like starting again? | Published | Keep as is. It is the practice group's anchor | Its thesis is now the site's hero line, which makes it the most load-bearing piece published |
| Issue 03, Not every criterion deserves equal effort | Ready | Revise before publication: move the three-question test earlier | Positioning brief section 7. The title is already in the right register; the body makes the reader wait for the payoff |
| Issue 04, Late reviews cannot change weak bids | Ready | Publish as approved. Add a route-one next step on the page | Strong pursuit-level piece, directly supports the reframed "Assure" step in the website audit section 4.4 |
| Issue 05, Your bid manager is expensive middleware | Ready | Publish as approved. Candidate for the first rung-3 close | Sponsor-primary, names a cost mechanism, currently stops at the functional consequence |
| Issue 06, Faster bids are not better decisions | Ready | Publish as approved. Best candidate to seed the maturity or MAP argument | Its thesis, that speed without better judgement is not advantage, is the argument MAP exists to answer |
| Post 15, You are paying twice for bid knowledge | Draft | Approve as the sponsor companion. It already reaches rung 3 | "It is lost bidding capacity" is the clearest economic bridge in the whole library |
| Post 16, I'm sure we have that somewhere | Draft | Approve as the champion companion | Five-question test, practical, correctly scoped |
| Post 17, Search is not enough for bid reuse | Draft | Approve, and treat it as the template for the new method or proof type | It is the only piece in the system that shows how Yorik tests rather than what he believes |
| Hub upcoming-issue teasers | Live | Correct to the approved titles, or remove the feature | They advertise two rejected titles (2.1) |
| Hub H1 | Live | Replace with the agreed copy in 5.1 | Repeats the homepage slogan (2.2) |

Nothing in the library needs retiring. That is worth saying plainly: six issues in, there
is no piece that contradicts the revised positioning or that Yorik would be embarrassed
by. The gap is what is missing, not what is wrong.

**What is missing, in priority order:**

1. A sector-anchored piece. Zero exist.
2. A long-form method or proof piece. Zero exist in long form.
3. A piece that teaches MAP. Zero exist.
4. A piece aimed at the technical gatekeeper, on data, security and control. Zero exist,
   and this is the role most likely to stop an engagement in a contractor or utility.

Those four are the natural spine of the next batch, if Yorik wants one planned.

## 7. Changes to the system

Small, and each one closes a gap this document found.

**Quality gate additions**, for `System/content-quality-standard.md`:

> 8. **Route.** Every piece belongs to route one (the live pursuit) or route two (the
>    practice behind it). Name it in the front matter. A piece that belongs to neither
>    does not enter a batch plan.
> 9. **ICP recognition.** Would a reader inside the defined ICP recognise this as being
>    about their world, or only about bidding in general? At least one piece in three
>    names a sector in the title or standfirst.

**Front matter additions** for long-form and short-form: `route`, `sector` (or `none`).

**Publication checklist addition** for `09_publication-prep`:

> Before preparing a website change, reconcile the hub page against `04_Ready/`. Titles,
> standfirsts and issue numbers on `insights.html` must match the approved files. Any
> forward-looking teaser is removed or corrected in the same change.

That single step would have caught 2.1.

**Batch plan addition** for `04_batch-planning`: state the route and reader mix for the
batch against the targets in 4.1 before drafting starts, so the balance is a plan rather
than an outcome.

## 8. The dormant weekday slots

`CONTENT-CONFIG.md` defines Monday long-form plus Wednesday sponsor, Thursday champion
and Friday proof. In practice only Monday is running, and three short-form companions sit
in draft.

This is an open item in the positioning brief and it is worth being clear about the
trade-off rather than defaulting to filling the calendar.

The case for filling the slots: the newsletter is the deep asset, but LinkedIn reach
comes from frequency, and posts 15, 16 and 17 already exist and are ready for review. The
existing design, one sponsor post, one champion post and one proof post per newsletter
theme, is sound and produces genuinely distinct pieces rather than three summaries.

The case for not filling them: gate 11 in `AGENTS.md` says to skip rather than force weak
material, and a four-a-week cadence run by one person alongside client delivery is the
most common way a content system quietly dies.

Middle path, and my recommendation: run the Monday newsletter plus **one** short-form
companion a week, alternating sponsor, champion and proof across the batch rather than
posting all three. That is two pieces a week, it keeps all three editorial jobs alive
across a batch, it uses the material already drafted, and it is sustainable. Revisit only
if `06_Learning/Analytics/` shows short-form is doing work the newsletter is not.

## 9. Measurement

The metrics list in `ignis-linkedin-content-strategy.md` is right and should stand. Three
additions specific to whether this realignment is working:

**Reader mix.** Who is actually subscribing. This is the Brand OS marketing action from
27 August and it is still unanswered. Until it is, every claim about whether the content
reaches sponsors is guesswork. It is one export from the newsletter platform.

**Route attribution.** When an enquiry arrives, record which article, if any, preceded it
and which route it came in on. The contact form already asks what the person wants to
discuss, so this costs one extra field or one note.

**Recognition, not reach.** The qualitative signal that matters is a sponsor or champion
saying some version of "this is us." Comments, DMs and replies that describe the reader's
own situation are the leading indicator that the ICP work is landing. Reach is not.

One post remains an observation. The existing rule holds.
