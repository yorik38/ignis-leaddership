AI roulette

Your organisation may be using AI. That does not mean it has adopted it.

AI tools are spreading faster than organisations are redesigning the work around them. People experiment at their desks. Corporate pilots demonstrate the technology. But the underlying workflow, evidence and decision rights often remain unchanged.

01

At the desk

The work still needs doing, so people use general AI tools for research, analysis and drafting. Prompting, evidence handling and checking vary by person, even while the organisation says AI is too risky for consequential commercial work.

02

At programme level

Central teams launch copilots and pilots. They demonstrate what the technology can produce, but without a commercial workflow owner and clear operating requirements they struggle to become something the business can trust and adopt.

Plenty of AI activity. Limited commercial impact.

That is AI roulette: experimentation without a commercially grounded design.

Enterprise research shows the same pilot-to-impact gap. McKinsey reports that most organisations have not embedded AI deeply enough into workflows to create material enterprise value. In ISG's study of 1,200 AI use cases, 31% had reached full production. OECD research identifies low AI maturity in procurement alongside the need for domain knowledge, data governance and collaboration.

What does Ignis Leadership do?

Ignis helps commercial, bid and procurement leaders improve how complex bids and tenders are run. We audit the work, redesign the division of work between people and AI agents, and install one governed capability on live work before scaling what proves useful.

What is a governed agentic AI system?

It is a shared operating capability in which AI agents coordinate or produce bounded work using approved knowledge, rules and controls. Named people retain judgement, commercial commitments and approval. The system keeps context, decisions and learning visible across the bid or tender lifecycle.

How is this different from using ChatGPT or Microsoft Copilot?

A chatbot helps an individual complete a task. A governed system works across a shared process. It uses agreed evidence, follows defined workflows, records what changed and brings decisions to the right people. The capability belongs to the organisation rather than remaining inside one person’s conversation.

Why is a governed agentic AI system well suited to commercial work?

Commercial teams spend much of their time preparing decisions rather than making them. Researching markets and suppliers, extracting requirements, comparing evidence, reconciling versions, producing decision packs and tracking actions all matter, but they can consume the people whose greatest value is judgement. A governed agentic system can carry this repeatable work across the process while named people retain strategy, relationships, negotiation, risk, approval and commitments. It does not replace commercial judgement. It gives people more time and better evidence to apply it.

Do we need to replace our existing tools or clean all our data first?

No. The first capability should work with the documents, cloud environment and enterprise tools the team already uses. The audit identifies missing evidence, inconsistent information and unclear ownership as part of the work. Start with the information needed for one bounded capability, not a complete transformation programme.

Where should a bid or tender team start with agentic AI?

Start with one pressure point in a live or recent bid, tender or transaction. Map where capacity, information or control breaks, select one bounded capability and agree how usefulness will be measured. Scale only if the pilot works.
