Every project starts by rebuilding the last one.

Most developers still run bids, tenders and route-to-market decisions on spreadsheets, inboxes and memory, so every project starts from scratch. Ignis helps you redesign the commercial function to work with agentic workflows built into your team.

Ignis starts with one live commercial workflow. We map where it slows down, which evidence and decisions matter, what the available data can support, and where risk needs human control.

We then redesign the work so agents handle bounded, repeatable tasks while your team manages exceptions, exercises judgement and makes commitments.

The result is not an agent added to a broken process. It is a better process that people and agents can run together, improve and reuse.

Straight answers, before you start.

Where should we start?

Start with one live commercial pressure or constrained workflow: a bid, portfolio decision, route-to-market problem, procurement programme or work needed for financial close. Ignis uses the real work to identify what must change in the process, data, decisions and human-agent handoffs.

What does the first engagement deliver?

Ignis maps the problem, process, decisions, evidence and data; identifies and prioritises useful agentic use cases; and designs the workflow, controls, evaluation and implementation plan. The exact outputs are agreed around the live pressure.

Who builds the technology?

Ignis leads the transformation design, requirements, human-agent workflow, governance, evaluation and adoption. Your technical team or selected delivery partners normally build and integrate production technology, with Ignis supporting implementation and protecting the commercial intent.

Do we need a new platform?

Not necessarily. Ignis starts with the tools, evidence and data your team already uses. New technology is considered only where it solves a defined problem and can be governed, evaluated and adopted properly.

How do we evaluate success?

Ignis agrees measures for the live workflow before implementation. These may include time spent on repeatable work, the quality and traceability of evidence, exceptions caught before approval, reuse of approved knowledge and whether people retain effective judgement and control.
