The problem

A process on paper. A person in practice.

Complex tenders aren't commodity RFPs, and "AI for bids" tools that stop at templates and search don't touch the real bottleneck. It sits in the gap between a defined process and the person executing it, since experience and capacity differ even across a fully qualified team, and it compounds every pursuit.

Your tenders follow a set process, but a process is only as consistent as whoever's running it. When you're managing several at once on tight margins, that inconsistency stops being small. It shows up directly in win rate, and in what's left on the job once you've won it.

Diagnose your bottlenecks →

  • Bid quality depends on who picks it up

    Your best judgement lives in a few heads, and experience varies even among people who are all qualified to run the bid. When someone moves to delivery, or leaves the business, it goes with them, and the next team rebuilds it from nothing.

  • Go/no-go gates take days to prepare

    Pulling together the case for an internal decision eats the early days of a bid, the exact time you need for positioning and strategy.

  • Nothing compounds between pursuits

    Winning answers and pricing logic from past bids exist somewhere. They're rarely captured in a way the next bid can actually use, so the same ground gets covered again.

  • Technical and commercial drift

    Sections are built in parallel by different people and stop matching by submission day.

  • Senior time lost to admin, not strategy

    Your most experienced people spend hours hunting for past answers and drafting boilerplate, time that should go into win themes and the case that actually wins the gate.
