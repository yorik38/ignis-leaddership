What this example shows

The install, the human decisions and the proof loop.

This is a composite example rather than a named client case study. It defines what would be measured without inventing a win rate or time-saving claim.

Choose the route

The switch changes the source material, the first controlled outcome, the human Gate and the measures. The governance pattern remains the same.

What enters

ITT, clarifications and approved evidence

A controlled source package and the existing bid timetable.

What FORGE prepares

Compliance and response matrix

Requirements, ownership, status, evidence needs, gaps and citations.

What people decide

Strategy, exceptions and sign-off

Go / No-Go, commercial positions, final wording and submission approval.

Controlled output

An owned, source-linked bid spine

A matrix ready for Gate review rather than a generated answer awaiting rescue.

01 / Trigger

The ITT lands.

The team opens a controlled project folder and confirms the source package and deadline.

02 / Structure

Requirements are extracted.

FORGE builds the compliance and response matrix with source references and mandatory checks.

03 / Ownership

Work is allocated.

Every requirement receives an owner, status and evidence need. Gaps become visible early.

04 / Assurance

Responses are checked.

The team sees completeness, citation coverage, exceptions and work awaiting a decision.

05 / Learning

Useful evidence returns.

Approved content and lessons can be reviewed for reuse in the next pursuit.

Named human Gate

People retain Go / No-Go, commercial strategy, exceptions, final response judgement and submission sign-off.

What the pilot measures

Client baselines determine whether the spine is worth expanding.

Matrix completenessAre all requirements captured and owned?
Citation coverageCan material claims be traced to trusted sources?
Time to first ownerHow quickly is each requirement allocated?
Gate outcomesAre decisions made with visible evidence?
What enters

Supplier returns, criteria and clarifications

The controlled return set, evaluation rules and decision timetable.

What FORGE prepares

Comparable evaluation matrix

Structured returns, mandatory checks, missing evidence and clarification status.

What people decide

Scores, exceptions and award

Evaluation judgement, moderation, commercial trade-offs and the award decision.

Controlled output

A traceable evaluation record

Comparable evidence and rationale ready for moderation and approval.

01 / Trigger

Supplier returns arrive.

The team confirms the controlled return set, evaluation rules and decision timetable.

02 / Structure

Returns become comparable.

FORGE builds one evaluation matrix and identifies mandatory checks and missing evidence.

03 / Clarify

Gaps are handled consistently.

Questions, responses and changes remain visible under the agreed equal-treatment protocol.

04 / Evaluate

Evidence supports judgement.

Evaluators see source-linked responses and exceptions without delegating scores to an agent.

05 / Learning

The sourcing record endures.

Approved rationale and lessons can be carried into the next tender cycle.

Named human Gate

People retain scoring judgement, clarification decisions, moderation, commercial exceptions and the award decision.

What the pilot measures

Client baselines determine whether the spine is worth expanding.

Evaluation completenessAre all criteria and returns covered?
Mandatory checksAre pass/fail requirements evidenced?
Clarification controlIs equal treatment visible in the record?
Decision traceabilityCan the award rationale be reconstructed?
What is installed

A small operating system for one project.

The pilot is configured in the team’s approved AI environment and project folder. It includes the selected protocol, controlled templates, naming and status rules, human gates and review measures.

The rest of the practice map stays dark. New workflows are connected only when the pilot evidence justifies them.
